# Appendix A — Foundations of Language Representation

This appendix answers one question: **how does text become something a computer can think with?**

At the lowest level, every language model is doing arithmetic. But language isn't arithmetic. "Cat" isn't a number. "The quick brown fox" isn't a matrix. So before any of the sophisticated stuff — attention, transformers, training — someone had to figure out how to bridge that gap. This appendix is about that bridge.

You don't need any prior experience with machine learning. If you know what a list of numbers is, you have enough background to follow everything here.

---

## What's in this appendix

**Chapter 1 — Why Computers Can't Understand Language**

The fundamental problem: computers work natively with numbers, but language is symbolic. We explore why the naive approach (ASCII codes) completely fails, what a *good* numerical representation of language would need to look like, and why this question is harder than it first appears.

**Chapter 2 — Tokens and Tokenization**

Before a model sees your text, it gets chopped into pieces called *tokens*. This chapter covers how and why — starting with the simplest possible approaches (character-level, word-level), showing exactly why each falls short, and building up to BPE (Byte Pair Encoding), the algorithm used by essentially every modern LLM. Includes an interactive notebook where you build all three tokenizers from scratch in Python.

**Chapter 3 — Embeddings: Giving Tokens Meaning**

Token IDs are still just arbitrary integers. Embeddings are the step that turns those integers into vectors with genuine semantic content — where "cat" and "kitten" end up mathematically close, and word arithmetic like `king − man + woman ≈ queen` actually works. This chapter explains how embeddings are stored, learned, and used, with a notebook demonstrating the geometry of meaning.

## A note on structure

Each chapter in this appendix is self-contained. You don't need to read Chapter 2 before Chapter 3 — though the concepts do naturally build on each other. If you're here to understand one specific thing, feel free to jump directly to it.

These chapters are also deliberately independent from the main content of the handbook. You can read them any time — before, during, or after the rest. They provide background that makes everything else cleaner to understand, but nothing in the main chapters requires you to have read these first.
